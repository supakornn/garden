Book by **Brian Tracy**.

> “The frog” is your hardest, most important task. Do it first.

My plain-language notes on the 21 rules from _Eat That Frog!_:

1. **Set the table:** Be clear about what you want to achieve. Unclear goals create hesitation and procrastination.
2. **Plan every day in advance:** Decide what you will do before the day starts. A written plan reduces wasted time and mental friction.
3. **Apply the 80/20 Rule:** A small number of tasks usually create most of the results. Focus on high-impact work instead of staying “busy.”
4. **Consider the consequences:** Important tasks usually have long-term benefits. Prioritize work that meaningfully improves your future.
5. **Practice creative procrastination:** Since you cannot do everything, intentionally delay or ignore low-value tasks.
6. **Use the ABCDE Method:** Categorize tasks by importance and complete “A” tasks before less important work.
7. **Focus on key result areas:** Identify the responsibilities that define success in your role or career.
8. **Apply the Law of Three:** Usually, three tasks contribute most of your results. Identify and prioritize them daily.
9. **Prepare thoroughly before you begin:** Gather tools, information, and resources beforehand to reduce resistance to starting.
10. **Take it one oil barrel at a time:** Break large goals into smaller, actionable steps.
11. **Upgrade your key skills:** Improving your most valuable skills increases confidence, speed, and effectiveness.
12. **Identify your key constraints:** Find the bottleneck slowing your progress and fix it first.
13. **Put pressure on yourself:** Set a deadline for yourself when nobody else has.
14. **Maximize your personal powers:** Productivity depends on energy, sleep, health, and mental clarity.
15. **Motivate yourself into action:** Focus on progress instead of excuses or negativity.
16. **Practice creative procrastination with technology:** Avoid distractions such as notifications, endless scrolling, and unnecessary context switching.
17. **Do the most difficult task first:** Tackle the hardest, most important task while your energy is highest.
18. **Slice and dice the task:** Start with a very small part of the task.
19. **Create large chunks of time:** Deep work needs uninterrupted focus. Protect long periods of concentration.
20. **Develop a sense of urgency:** Act decisively and avoid unnecessary delays.
21. **Single-handle every task:** Once you start an important task, continue until it is meaningfully completed.
